VetData design system · v1.1

Calm software for busy exam rooms.

VetData helps veterinarians run their day: patients, records, schedule, lab results and an AI assistant that drafts notes and checks doses. The interface is papery and quiet. One warm serif sets every word, controls are pill-shaped and mostly flat, depth comes from fill shifts and hairlines, and lemon yellow marks the single primary action on each screen. Items marked imagined were extrapolated from the source screenshots.

TypefaceSerif everywhere. Literata, opsz on.
BrandForest #2A3F2E, dog-head logo tile.
SurfaceCream #F8F5EA, never pure white pages.
AccentLemon #F3DC7E, one primary per view.
ShapePills for controls, 6px chips, 14px cards.
IconsVetData Icons, 24 grid, 1.75 stroke.

Do

  • Use the serif for every word, including buttons, chips, table cells and the composer.
  • Keep one lemon button per view. Other actions are forest, cream, white-outline or ghost.
  • Always show units next to clinical numbers: 28.4 kg, 38.6 °C, 0.1 mg/kg.
  • Put patient alerts (allergies, behavior) in the patient header, before anything else.
  • Align variants on a shared left edge and keep numbers right-aligned and tabular.

Don't

  • Don't use a sans-serif for UI text (mono only for IDs, microchips and code).
  • Don't use pure white surfaces. White is for the outline button and form controls.
  • Don't show 0 for a missing value: use an em dash.
  • Don't add shadows to resting cards or buttons.
  • Don't use lemon for text or thin lines; it fails contrast on cream.
Foundations

Three families carry the interface: forest for brand and ink, cream for surfaces and borders (warm, with a faint green bias) and lemon as the one accent. Semantic, clinical-range and chart colors are imagined and kept muted so they sit on cream.

Typography

One serif family does everything: a warm text serif with sturdy, slightly slabbed serifs and a large x-height. Literata (variable, optical sizing) is the match; Merriweather is the fallback. Headings are semibold, body is regular, and clinical numbers are always tabular.

Assistant · new

Assistant mascot · Pip

Pip is the face of the VetData assistant: a frosted cream dog or cat head, adapted from the Frost Cloud pattern. Soft shapes melt into one silhouette through a "goo" filter, the eyes, nose and mouth are drawn in forest, and the backdrop is forest or lemon. Pip floats and breathes, glances around, blinks, twitches an ear now and then, follows your pointer with its eyes, and squishes, perks its ears and winks when clicked. Try it on the tiles below.

Anatomy · viewBox 240

  • Dog · round head, two jowls, floppy ears tilted ±16° with a lemon tint, pill eyes 16×27, oval nose, "w" mouth.
  • Cat · wide head, two cheeks, triangle ears with lemon insides, pill eyes 16×25, triangle nose, whiskers.
  • Body · cream #FCFAF4 → #EEE9D8, backdrop shining through at the bottom, white shine top-left.
  • Features · forest #2A3F2E → #3A5640 with a cream glint in each eye.
  • Backdrops · forest with a lemon glow, or lemon with a forest outline.

Motion

  • Floats ±5px and breathes ±1.2% on soft springs.
  • Glances every 1.4–3.6 s, blinks every 2.4–5.4 s, twitches an ear every 3–7 s.
  • Eyes follow the pointer; a click squishes, perks the ears and winks.
  • Paused off-screen. Reduced motion: still pose, a click bounces once.

Where Pip appears

  • Assistant empty state · 96–160px, no backdrop (see Conversation).
  • Onboarding "Assistant" step and first run · 200–320px with backdrop.
  • Long assistant work · next to "Reading Bella's last 3 visits".
  • Empty states that invite people to ask the assistant.

Where Pip never appears

  • As the 28px message avatar (keep the sparkle avatar).
  • In clinical data views, alerts, dialogs or invoices.
  • Next to bad news: a deceased patient, critical results, failed payments.
  • More than once per screen.

Dog by default. Clinics can switch the assistant to the cat in Settings → Assistant.
